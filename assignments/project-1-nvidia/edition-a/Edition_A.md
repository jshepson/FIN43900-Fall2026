## Synthetic enterprise-to-equity bridge check

```text
Equity value = Enterprise value + cash - debt - non-controlling interest - pension claim
Equity value = 1,000 + 100 - 250 - 20 - 10 = 820
Per-share value = 820 / 100 diluted shares = $8.20
```

This check confirms the required sequence: value operations for all capital providers, add non-operating assets, subtract debt and other non-common claims, then divide common-equity value by a stated diluted-share convention.

## Consequential assumptions

- AI infrastructure demand will remain sufficiently strong to support continued Data Center growth after the current platform ramp.
- Gross margin can stabilize at a level consistent with the cost of increasingly complex data-center systems and recurring product transitions.
- NVIDIA can manage supply and capacity commitments without material inventory write-downs or excess capacity if customer demand slows.
- Cash, debt, marketable securities, lease obligations, investments, and any other non-operating assets or claims can be classified consistently in the eventual enterprise-to-equity bridge.

## Unknowns and potential disconfirming evidence

- The market price as of the valuation date may already embed more revenue growth and margin durability than a supportable valuation produces.
- Data Center growth may normalize sharply once customers complete an initial AI-infrastructure buildout.
- Export controls, China restrictions, customer concentration, competition, supply constraints, and product-transition execution could weaken revenue growth or margins.
- A valuation-date diluted share count may differ materially from the FY2026 weighted-average EPS denominator.

## Research and validation plan

1. Record the market price and common shares outstanding as of the valuation date, then choose and document a consistent diluted-share convention.
2. Build a source-labeled enterprise-to-equity bridge from the Form 10-K and identify whether marketable securities, leases, investments, or other claims require treatment beyond cash and debt.
3. Forecast revenue, operating margin, taxes, reinvestment, and FCFF using base, upside, and downside cases.
4. Test a downside case with slower Data Center growth, lower gross margin, and weaker customer demand.
5. Compare the implied per-share valuation range with the market price on the valuation date.
6. Validate consequential inputs against primary SEC filings and record units, reporting periods, classifications, and source locators for every load-bearing value.
