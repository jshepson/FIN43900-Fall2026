Video 2 - Code and Validation

This is my final NVIDIA Project 1 valuation application. I run analysis.py from the repository root. The program uses the five-year three-statement pro-forma already developed for NVIDIA and converts the operating forecast into free cash flow to the firm, or FCFF.

The starting historical period is FY2026. The main company-specific driver is the Data Center ramp. In the base case, revenue growth is front-loaded and then fades over the five-year forecast: 70 percent, 40 percent, 25 percent, 15 percent, and 8 percent. Gross margin starts at 74 percent and gradually declines to 72.5 percent. Those are assumptions, not filing facts, and I identify the evidence that could change them in my assumption-challenge record.

The code calculates FCFF from operating income after tax, then adds depreciation and amortization, subtracts capital spending, and subtracts the change in operating net working capital. It discounts each annual FCFF at the selected WACC. It also calculates a terminal value after year five using the Gordon-growth formula.

The code then converts enterprise value to equity value. It adds 62,556 million dollars of cash and marketable securities and subtracts 8,468 million dollars of debt. Finally, it divides by 24,304 million common shares. I state separately that this share proxy is not the same as the historical weighted-average diluted EPS denominator.

The output shows three causal cases. The low case is 137.31 dollars per share. It combines five percentage points lower revenue growth in each forecast year, 100 basis points lower gross margin, a 10.75 percent WACC, and 2.5 percent terminal growth. The base case is 193.14 dollars per share. The high case is 280.88 dollars per share, but it requires stronger growth and margins plus more favorable discount-rate and terminal-growth assumptions.

The current market reference is 221.70 dollars. The model also runs a reverse DCF. Holding base margin, WACC, and terminal growth constant, the market reference requires about a 4.13 percentage-point increase to every explicit revenue-growth assumption. That is not proof the market is wrong; it identifies what expectation needs to be supported by future evidence.

My validation includes a known-answer enterprise-to-equity bridge. The synthetic example starts with enterprise value of 1,000, adds 100 of cash, subtracts 250 of debt, 20 of non-controlling interest, and 10 of pension claims. Equity value is 820, or 8.20 per share using 100 diluted shares. The program also checks the FY2026 base-year reconciliation, forecast balance-sheet articulation, calculated cash, enterprise-to-equity bridge arithmetic, WACC monotonicity, and terminal-growth monotonicity.

For my locked changed-input record, I changed only WACC from 10 percent to 11 percent. I predicted that value would increase. That prediction was wrong. Holding the same forecast cash flows and all other inputs constant, value declined from 193.14 to 167.82 dollars per share, a decrease of 25.33 dollars. A higher required return discounts the same future cash flows more heavily. The result strengthens the watch/defer conclusion. The largest remaining model limitation is terminal value, which is 77 percent of base enterprise value, so I disclose it rather than treating the base output as a precise answer.
