Video 3 - Committee Defense

My recommendation to the investment committee is watch/defer NVIDIA. I do not recommend initiating a position at the September 24, 2026 market reference of 221.70 dollars per share. My valuation range is 137.31 to 280.88 dollars per share, and my base case is 193.14 dollars per share.

The recommendation is not based on a claim that NVIDIA is a weak company. The operating evidence is strong. NVIDIA's Data Center business is the major value driver, and recent reported growth and margin evidence support continued research attention. The question is whether the market price already incorporates more future growth, margin durability, and cash-flow conversion than my supportable base assumptions deliver.

The model's base case uses a front-loaded revenue-growth path that declines from 70 percent in the first forecast year to 8 percent in the fifth. It uses gross margins from 74 percent down to 72.5 percent, a 10 percent WACC, and 3 percent terminal growth. These assumptions produce 193.14 dollars per share. The low case produces 137.31 dollars by combining a slower demand path, lower margins, higher WACC, and lower terminal growth. The high case produces 280.88 dollars, but that outcome requires multiple favorable changes at the same time.

I do not select the high value simply because it is above the market reference. The high case is conditional, not my central expectation. The reverse DCF shows that the market reference requires about 4.13 percentage points of additional growth in every explicit forecast year, holding the base margin, WACC, and terminal-growth assumptions constant. That makes the required operating outcome concrete and gives the committee a monitoring question.

My largest risk is the durability of the Data Center ramp. NVIDIA's customers must continue to fund and deploy AI infrastructure, and NVIDIA must manage supply commitments, product transitions, customer concentration, China restrictions, and margin pressure. Higher capital intensity or weaker cash conversion would reduce FCFF even if reported revenue remains high.

The model has important limitations. Terminal value represents 77 percent of base enterprise value, so small changes to long-run assumptions can have material effects. The AMD historical P/E result is also not averaged with my DCF because it is a single qualified peer with a different business mix and a historically based earnings denominator. It is useful as a challenge, but it is not a defensible separate decision range.

I would reconsider an initiation if two reported quarters support the modeled Data Center demand, gross margin, and operating-cash-flow conversion, and if the price provides a margin of safety compared with the base valuation. I would move toward do-not-initiate if reported growth, margin, cash conversion, or the enterprise-to-equity bridge deteriorates materially. My committee ask is to retain NVIDIA on watch/defer, monitor the next filing, and refresh the valuation before committing capital.
