## Presentation: selection to valuation conclusion

I chose NVIDIA because it is a clear public-company case of an AI-infrastructure boom: its Data Center business was rapidly expanding, but the valuation depends on whether that extraordinary demand can persist as the revenue base grows. It therefore has strong primary-source evidence, measurable operating drivers, and a genuine question about whether operating success is already reflected in the share price.

The key source supporting the near-term demand assumption is NVIDIA's Q2 FY2027 Form 10-Q. It reports Data Center revenue of $89.0 billion, up 117% year over year and 18% sequentially. Management guided Q3 FY2027 revenue to $108.0 billion plus or minus 2%, and stated a preliminary expectation of about 70% FY2028 revenue growth in its August 2026 earnings call. This supports the first high-growth forecast year. The later growth path--40%, 25%, 15%, and 8%--is my judgmental fade as the revenue base expands and supply capacity, customer concentration, China restrictions, customer deployment capacity, and product transitions become more important.

My pro-forma forecasts revenue growth of 70%, 40%, 25%, 15%, and 8% for FY2027E through FY2031E, with gross margins of 74.0%, 74.0%, 73.5%, 73.0%, and 72.5%. The model links revenue and margins to operating income, working capital, cash flow, and the balance sheet; the balance-sheet gap is $0.0m in the base case.

The FCFE equity valuation uses a 10.0% cost of equity, 3.0% terminal growth, and 24,304m shares. It produces a base value of **$190.81 per share**, compared with the **$221.70 per-share market reference on September 24, 2026**. My conclusion is **watch/defer** because the base case does not provide a margin of safety.

My earlier FCFF DCF produced $176.67 per diluted share on September 10, 2026. It differs because it uses a different cash-flow measure, enterprise-to-equity bridge, share count, valuation date, and modeling approach. I do not average the methods. I do not have a completed NVIDIA peer valuation to use as a decision value; that is a limitation, not a value to invent.

## Sensitivity interpretation

Revenue growth is the larger tested driver. Changing each annual growth assumption by plus or minus 5 percentage points changes value from $160.38 to $225.76 per share, a $65.38 span. Changing gross margin by plus or minus 1 percentage point changes value from $187.60 to $194.01 per share, a $6.41 span. The mechanism is revenue growth to revenue, gross profit and operating income, FCFE, and equity value.

This ranking is conditional on the tested ranges. It does not establish that growth is inherently more important than margin, and it does not assign probabilities to the cases.

The evidence most likely to change my conclusion is evidence on the durability and fade rate of the Data Center ramp: customer demand and deployment, supply capacity and commitments, customer concentration, gross-margin execution through product transitions, and China restrictions.

## Reviewer questions prepared for NVIDIA analysis

### Selection and evidence

**Question:** Why did you choose NVIDIA, and what source best supports your claim that Data Center demand can support your forecast?

**Example answer:** I chose NVIDIA because its AI and Data Center growth creates a meaningful valuation question: strong results do not automatically mean the share price is supported. The Q2 FY2027 10-Q reports $89.0 billion in Data Center revenue, up 117% year over year. Q3 revenue guidance and management's preliminary expectation of about 70% FY2028 revenue growth support the near-term forecast; the later years are a judgmental fade.

**Question:** What evidence would make you reduce the 70%, 40%, 25%, 15%, and 8% revenue-growth path?

**Example answer:** I would reduce the path if quarterly Data Center revenue growth slows materially, if customers delay deployments because of power, land, or financing constraints, or if supply commitments and inventory rise faster than cash conversion. I would also reassess it if China restrictions, customer concentration, or a product transition limits sales.

### Model and valuation

**Question:** Show me how your revenue-growth assumption flows through the model to per-share value. Where would a faster demand fade reduce FCFE?

**Example answer:** Lower growth reduces forecast revenue. With margin held constant, gross profit and operating income fall. Lower income reduces cash from operations, while working-capital balances such as receivables and inventory can still use cash. After capex and shareholder returns, FCFE falls. Discounting lower FCFE produces a lower equity value per share. The base case produces $190.81 per share.

**Question:** Why does your earlier FCFF DCF value differ from the $190.81 FCFE pro-forma value, and why do you not average them?

**Example answer:** The FCFF DCF was valued on September 10, 2026 and produced $176.67 per diluted share. The pro-forma FCFE valuation was valued on September 24, uses a different cash-flow measure, a different enterprise-to-equity treatment, and a different share count. They are separate conditional analyses, so averaging them would hide the reason for the difference rather than resolve it.

### Sensitivity and interpretation

**Question:** Why does growth rank as a larger driver than gross margin in your sensitivity, and could the ranking change?

**Example answer:** Growth has the larger span because I changed growth by plus or minus 5 percentage points in every forecast year, but changed gross margin by only plus or minus 1 percentage point. Growth produced a $65.38 per-share span versus $6.41 for margin. The ranking is conditional on those ranges; a wider, evidence-supported margin range could change it.

**Question:** Why is the higher growth case not proof that NVIDIA is undervalued?

**Example answer:** The $225.76 higher-growth value is conditional on all five annual growth inputs being 5 percentage points above the base case while the other independent assumptions remain at base. The sensitivity assigns no probability to that path and does not capture correlation with margins, working capital, or capital spending. It shows what the model does if that condition holds; it is not a forecast of what will happen.

## Additional presenter and reviewer discussion examples

### Selection and evidence

**Reviewer question:** What source specifically supports your most important revenue-growth assumption, and which part of the forecast is your own judgment?

**Example answer:** My main source is NVIDIA's Q2 FY2027 10-Q, which reports $89.0 billion of Data Center revenue, up 117% year over year. Management also guided Q3 revenue to $108.0 billion plus or minus 2% and discussed about 70% FY2028 growth. That supports the near-term high-growth assumption. The later fade--40%, 25%, 15%, and 8%--is my judgment because management does not provide a five-year forecast.

### Model and valuation

**Reviewer question:** Can you trace slower Data Center growth from revenue to FCFE and value per share?

**Example answer:** Slower growth lowers forecast revenue. At the same gross-margin assumption, gross profit and operating income decrease. Lower income reduces operating cash generation; meanwhile, inventory and receivables can still use cash through working capital. After capex and shareholder returns, FCFE is lower. Discounting that lower FCFE produces a lower equity value per share.

### Sensitivity and interpretation

**Reviewer question:** Does your main driver rank first because of business economics, or partly because you tested it over a wider range?

**Example answer:** Both matter. Revenue growth is economically important because it compounds over five years and changes the revenue base that produces profit and cash flow. But the size of the ranking also depends on my ranges: I tested growth at plus or minus 5 percentage points each year, while margins moved only plus or minus 1 point. Therefore, the sensitivity proves the growth path has the larger effect over those specific ranges; it does not prove it is always more important than margin.

### Evidence that could change the conclusion

**Reviewer question:** What next-quarter evidence would change your conclusion, and what part of the model would it affect?

**Example answer:** I would watch Data Center growth, gross-margin guidance and results, customer concentration, supply commitments, and China exposure. Stronger, durable demand with stable margins could justify raising the growth path or margins and increase FCFE and value. Evidence of weaker deployment demand, margin pressure, or supply commitments not converting into cash could lower revenue or margins, increase working-capital needs, reduce FCFE, and reinforce the watch/defer conclusion.
